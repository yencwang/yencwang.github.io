About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Archaeology program at UCLA. My dissertation examines sugar craftwork in Early Modern Taiwan from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries. By using the distribution patterns of zisha teaware at non-elite sites including settlements associated with sugar manufactories, my research challenges the notion that the Late Imperial Chinese economy had become trapped in a cycle of involution, which economic historians often used to explain the lack of technological improvement in production systems. More broadly, my research interests include premodern interactions across East and Southeast Asia, with a particular focus on social change and continuity during periods of technological transformation, including developments in agriculture and iron metallurgy.
